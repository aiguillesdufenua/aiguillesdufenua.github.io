Using an automatic watch

Winding the movement, setting the time and date, and the few things to avoid so your watch stays with you for a long time.

How it works

An automatic watch has no battery. Its energy is stored in a spring, the mainspring, which slowly unwinds to move the hands.

To wind that spring, a half-disc called the “rotor” spins freely inside the watch with every movement of your wrist. Worn every day, the watch winds itself.

Once you take it off, it keeps running for its “power reserve”, often around 40 hours depending on the movement. If it stays in a drawer longer than that, it stops: that’s normal, just wind it and set it again.

The crown and its positions

The crown is the small knob on the side of the case, usually at 3 o’clock. Depending on whether it is pushed in or pulled out one or two clicks, it does different things.

Screw-down crown? On some watches, the crown screws onto the case for better water protection. First unscrew it by turning it towards you (counterclockwise) until it releases. After each adjustment, screw it back in while pressing lightly, without forcing.

Winding the watch

If your watch has stopped, or you haven’t worn it for a few days, give it some energy by hand before setting it.

  1. Leave the crown in position 0 (pushed in, or unscrewed if it screws down).
  2. Gently turn it upwards, towards 12 o’clock, about twenty to thirty times. The hands should start moving again.
  3. Then wear the watch: the rotor will take over.

You won’t feel a hard stop: an automatic watch is designed so it can’t be overwound, the spring simply slips once it’s full. Turn gently, there’s no need to keep going.

Some movements can’t be wound by hand. In that case, gently swing the watch from side to side for a minute to get it going.

Setting the time

Movement without date

  1. Pull the crown out one click (position 1). On many movements, the seconds hand stops: handy for setting the time to the second.
  2. Turn the crown to move the hands to the right time. For more precision, go slightly past the time, then come back to the right minute.
  3. Push the crown back to position 0 on a round second, then screw it down if needed.

Movement with date

The principle is the same, but the time is set by pulling the crown out two clicks (position 2). With a date, you also need to know whether it’s morning or afternoon: the date changes at midnight, not at noon. The full method is just below.

Setting the date

Never do this

Never change the date (crown in position 1) while the hour and minute hands are in the upper part of the dial, between 9 and 3.

Around midnight, the date mechanism is already switching to the next day. Forcing the date at that moment can damage or break the small teeth of that mechanism. On the dial, you can’t tell whether it’s 10 in the morning or 10 at night, so the simple rule is to avoid that whole zone.

The safe method, step by step

  1. Pull the crown out two clicks (position 2) and move the hands to around 6:30, in the safe zone.
  2. Push it in one click (position 1). Turn it to set the date to the day before today. Depending on the movement, only one direction moves the date: if nothing happens, turn the other way.
  3. Pull it back out to position 2 and move the hands forward until the date changes to today. You now know it’s midnight.
  4. Keep moving the hands forward to the exact time, adding 12 hours if it’s the afternoon.
  5. Push the crown back to position 0 and screw it down if it’s a screw-down crown.

30-day months and February: the watch doesn’t know the calendar, it always counts up to 31. On the first day after a short month, simply move the date forward one or more days, always with the hands in the safe zone.

Good habits

  • Take the watch off to set it. Handling the crown on your wrist puts sideways pressure on the stem.
  • Always push the crown back in, and screw it down if it screws, before getting your hands wet or going into the water. Never operate it underwater.
  • Keep it away from magnets: speakers, magnetic clasps on bags or phone cases. A magnetized watch often starts running very fast.
  • Avoid hard knocks, such as a fall onto tiles or certain sports.
  • Running a little fast or slow is normal: a mechanical watch can gain or lose a few seconds a day, sometimes more depending on the movement.
  • A service now and then by a watchmaker cleans and lubricates the movement so it lasts a long time.
