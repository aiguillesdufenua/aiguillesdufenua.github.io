It’s … in Tahiti

My passion

Watchmaking, lived from Tahiti, at the pace of the fenua, our homeland.

It all started with curiosity: wanting to understand what makes a watch tick, behind the dial.

That curiosity grew into a passion, one I’m learning step by step. Today, I choose the components of my watches and put them together myself: the movement, the dial, the hands, the case. It teaches me the patience every screw demands, and every hand set in exactly the right place.

With each new watch, I push my technique a little further. My goal is to one day take apart and reassemble an entire movement. I’m not there yet, and that’s exactly what makes the journey so exciting.

Les Aiguilles du Fenua (“the hands of the fenua”) is the name I gave this passion. It isn’t a job or a business: I make watches for the joy of it, watches that reflect who I am and reflect our island: the deep blue of the lagoon, the dark green of the mountains, the golden light at the end of the day, the tiare flower at the heart of my emblem.

I wear them, and sometimes I give them to people I care about. I’m not trying to make many, but to make them well: one-of-a-kind pieces that take the time they need.

How it all began

From collecting to creating

“The pleasure is no longer fleeting.”

For years, my relationship with watchmaking came down to the joy of discovering and acquiring. I gathered many beautiful pieces, each carefully chosen to mark a milestone, celebrate an important moment in life, or simply for the thrill of wearing a new, exceptional object.

Yet I eventually felt the limits of this approach. The excitement of something new was short-lived. The pleasure, however intense, remained instantaneous, bound to the act of buying or to the event it celebrated. Once the watch became part of everyday life, the magic of the first days gently faded and gave way to habit.

Something deeper was missing: an intimate bond with the material and the mechanism. So I made myself a promise. For my next watches, I no longer wanted to simply choose them in a shop window; I wanted to bring them to life. That is how this adventure began.

Moving to the other side of the workbench has completely changed the way I see time. Studying the architecture of calibres, pairing the textures of a dial, using the setting tool to fit the delicate hour hand with infinite care… Assembly demands a precision and focus that anchor me entirely in the present moment. The pleasure is no longer fleeting. It now lives in the hours of patience, in the rigour of handwork, and in the deep satisfaction of having brought a unique piece to life with my own hands.

From idea to wrist

Every watch follows the same path, by hand, at my own pace and with no shortcuts.

  1. The idea

    A wish, a color, a memory, or the person close to me I have in mind while creating it.

  2. The movement

    I choose the heart of the watch from existing movements, based on the style I’m after and how it will be worn.

  3. The dial and hands

    Colors, shapes, finishes: this is where the piece takes on its character, often inspired by the fenua.

  4. Assembly

    I fit each part with care. This is the step where I learn the most, and where I try a new technique every time.

  5. The card

    To keep a record of each piece, I pair it with a small numbered and signed card.

One piece, one story

No two of my watches are alike.

Each one has its own card that keeps its memory: its number, its model, its movement, the date and my signature. A small hobbyist’s ritual. The card itself is written in French.
