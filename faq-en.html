Everyday life

My watch has stopped: is it broken?

Most likely not. An automatic watch stops when it isn’t worn for longer than its power reserve, often around 40 hours. Wind it about twenty turns of the crown, set the time and wear it: it will start again.

If it stops while being worn every day, it needs to be checked by a watchmaker.

Do I need to wind it every day?

No, if you wear it every day: the movements of your wrist are enough to wind it through its rotor. Hand-winding is only useful if it has stopped or if you’re not very active, for example at a desk all day.

Can I overwind it?

No. The spring of an automatic watch simply slips once it’s full; you won’t even feel a stop. There’s no need to turn the crown endlessly, though: twenty to thirty turns are enough.

My watch gains (or loses) a few seconds a day: is that normal?

Yes. A mechanical watch isn’t as accurate as a quartz. For the calibres in my watches, the manufacturer quotes between −20 and +40 seconds a day; in practice they often do much better. Resetting your watch now and then is part of the charm.

It suddenly started gaining several minutes a day. Why?

It is often a sign that it has been magnetised, by a speaker, a tablet, or the magnetic clasp of a bag or phone case. The coils of the hairspring then stick together. A watchmaker can demagnetise it in seconds with a special device.

What should I do with my watch at night?

Just put it down. A little tip: its position affects its rate. Depending on the watch, lying dial up or crown down, it will run a little faster or slower. By watching it for a few nights, you can choose the position that offsets its daytime deviation.

Is a watch winder useful?

Not essential. It’s a small motorised box that turns the watch to keep it wound when you’re not wearing it. Handy if you own several automatic watches, or a complication that’s long to set, like a perpetual calendar. For a watch worn regularly, it isn’t necessary.

Can I wear it while doing sport?

Sports with hard impacts, like tennis, golf, mountain biking or combat sports, are tough on the movement: better take it off. For walking, running or gentle cycling, no problem.

Settings

Why shouldn’t I set the date between 9 and 3?

Around midnight, the date mechanism is already switching to the next day; forcing the date at that moment can damage its small teeth. Since you can’t tell on the dial whether it’s 10 in the morning or the evening, the simple rule is to avoid the whole upper half of the dial. The full method is in the user guide.

The date changes at noon instead of midnight: is my watch faulty?

No: it was simply set twelve hours off. Move the hands forward twelve hours, one full turn of the dial: the date will now change at midnight.

Can I turn the hands backwards?

On most modern movements, going back a few minutes is harmless. Just avoid going backwards through the date-change zone around midnight, and move forward when you can.

What should I do after a 30-day month or after February?

A simple date always counts to 31. On the first day of the next month, just move the date forward one or more clicks, with the hands in the lower half of the dial.

Water and climate

Can I swim with my watch?

It depends on its water resistance, shown in metres or bar, and on the condition of its gaskets. Count on at least 100 metres to swim with peace of mind and 200 metres to dive; a “30 m” watch only handles splashes. The markings are explained on the Technologies page.

For my home-assembled watches, the answer is no: without a pressure test, I don’t take them in the water.

Fog has appeared under the crystal. What should I do?

It means moisture has got into the case, often through a tired gasket or a crown left open. Don’t leave it: moisture rusts the parts of the movement. Take it to a watchmaker quickly.

Should I rinse my watch after the sea?

Yes, it’s the right habit in the tropics: a rinse in fresh water, crown screwed down, stops salt from building up in the links, the bezel and the gaskets.

Is a leather strap a good idea in Tahiti?

For special occasions, yes; every day, less so. Heat, humidity, sweat and seawater deform it and can make it mouldy. Steel, titanium, fluoroelastomer (FKM) rubber and NATO straps suit our climate much better.

Maintenance

How often should an automatic watch be serviced?

Roughly every four to six years. The watchmaker takes the movement apart, cleans and oils it, and replaces the gaskets. In our hot, humid climate, oils and gaskets age a little faster: better not to wait too long.

How do I clean a steel bracelet?

With lukewarm water, a little mild soap, and a soft toothbrush for the links. Rinse, then dry with a soft cloth. With the crown closed, of course.

Can I change the strap myself?

Yes, it’s the simplest job. The strap is held between the lugs by spring bars, which a small forked tool compresses. You just need to know your watch’s lug width, for example 20 mm, to choose the right strap.

Can scratches on the crystal be removed?

On an acrylic crystal, yes: polishing paste makes them disappear. On a mineral crystal, it’s difficult. On a practically scratch-proof sapphire crystal, a scratch means replacing the crystal.

Getting started

I’d like to assemble my own watch: where do I start?

With a complete kit, as I did for my first watch: all the parts are designed to go together, and you learn the skills without worrying about compatibility. The Making page details the basic tools and steps.

Do I need to be a watchmaker to assemble a watch?

No. Assembling existing components mainly takes patience, care, good tools and a clean space. Taking apart and repairing a movement is another story, learned step by step. It’s actually one of my goals.

What tools do I need at minimum?

Watchmaker’s screwdrivers, tweezers, a hand setter, a wrench or press for the case back, an air blower, finger cots and a mat. A loupe and some Rodico putty quickly prove very useful.
